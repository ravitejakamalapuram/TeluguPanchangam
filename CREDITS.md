# Credits and Sources

The daily Gita verse in Telugu Panchangam bundles the complete Bhagavad Gita (shared with Gita Wisdom New Tab): 18 chapters, 701 verses. Every text shipped in
`gita-verses.js` is in the public domain or under a public-domain dedication. The extension bundles no
modern copyrighted translation, commentary or word-by-word gloss.

| Content | Source | Licence / status |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Sanskrit (Devanagari)** | [`gita/gita`](https://github.com/gita/gita), file `data/verse.json`, field `text` (commit `700c015`, 2022-10-22) | [The Unlicense](https://github.com/gita/gita/blob/main/LICENSE) (public-domain dedication). The Sanskrit text itself is ancient and in the public domain. |
| **English translation** | Annie Besant, *The Bhagavad-Gita* (4th edition, Theosophical Publishing House, 1922), as transcribed on Wikisource: <https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Bhagavad-Gita_(Besant_4th)> | Public domain. It was published in 1922, and Besant died in 1933. Wikisource tags it `PD-old` for the original text and `PD/US` (1933) for the translation. |
| **Chapter titles** | Besant's closing titles for each discourse (for example, "The Yoga of Action") | Public domain (same edition) |
| **Transliteration (IAST)** | Generated by `scripts/build-verses.py`, which converts the Devanagari above character by character | Not a translation, and derived only from the public-domain text above |

## What was *not* used

- `gita/gita` also ships `transliteration`, `word_meanings`, `translation.json` and `commentary.json`.
  These come from modern translators and commentators (for example Swami Sivananda, Swami Gambirananda
  and Swami Mukundananda) whose copyright is not covered by the repository's Unlicense. They are
  **not** used.
- The English translation, word meanings, Hindi translation and commentary in earlier versions (≤ 1.0.2)
  were removed in 1.1.0 because their provenance could not be shown to be public domain.

## Why Besant rather than Telang

K. T. Telang's 1882 translation (*Sacred Books of the East*, vol. 8) is also public domain. However, it is
continuous prose with no verse numbers, so it cannot be reliably aligned to chapter:verse. Besant's
edition prints the Sanskrit and English verse by verse, with explicit verse numbers. Its per-chapter
verse counts match the Sanskrit exactly (47, 72, 43, 42, 29, 47, 30, 28, 34, 42, 55, 20, 35, 27, 20, 24,
28, 78 = 701).

## Editorial changes

- Besant's footnotes are omitted.
- The Sanskrit verse-number markers (`।।2.47।।`) are normalised to `॥`, and half-verses are split onto
  separate lines.
- A legacy-font artefact in the source Sanskrit is corrected: an i-vowel sign placed inside a conjunct,
  for example `श्िच` corrected to `श्चि`.
- Four verse markers that were mis-transcribed on Wikisource are renumbered sequentially. These are
  10.31, 11.8, 17.19 and 18.14; each was checked against the Sanskrit.

## Machine translations

Languages other than English are machine-translated from Besant's English text on demand, entirely
on-device through Chrome's built-in Translator API, and cached locally. They are not bundled. If the
on-device Translator isn't available (unsupported Chrome version, missing language pack), the bundled
English text is shown instead — no verse text is ever sent to a remote translation server.

## Rebuilding

See the docstring at the top of `scripts/build-verses.py` for how to download the inputs and regenerate
`gita-verses.js`.
